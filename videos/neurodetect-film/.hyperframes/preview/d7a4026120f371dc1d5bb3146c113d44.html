NeuroDetect
Making Brain-Wave Screening More Accessible.
Vedant Shinde  |  Rhythm Tyagi
MIT WORLD PEACE UNIVERSITY, PUNE
The brain never
stops talking.
Billions of cells,
signalling in tiny pulses of electricity.
Those signals leave a trace.
Can we learn to read them?
Alzheimer's disease is the
most common cause of dementia,
and it is often
recognised late.
The tests that confirm it are
costly, and specialists
are scarce.
In low-income countries,
there is about one
neurology specialist for every million people.
An EEG is
different.
It is cheap,
painless, and already
in most hospitals.
NeuroDetect reads one short,
resting EEG recording.
It needs just five of the usual
nineteen sensors.
Three for Alzheimer's-type changes,
and two for a mood marker.
Here is the journey of one
recording.
The signal is filtered
to between half a hertz
and forty-five hertz,
and resampled to two hundred and
fifty samples a second.
Then it is cut into
ten-second slices.
Slices spoiled by blinks or movement
are thrown away.
Every clean slice
becomes a few numbers,
called biomarkers, and each slice
is judged on its own.
Two paths run side
by side.
The Alzheimer's path compares slow theta
waves with alpha waves
at the back of the head,
and checks how closely the front
and back of the brain
stay in step.
The second path
measures frontal alpha asymmetry,
a depression-related marker.
It is preliminary,
and shown as indicative
only.
The classifier is deliberately
simple.
It learns one typical
healthy pattern and one typical
Alzheimer's pattern, and asks
which a slice is closer
to, using cosine distance.
Every slice casts a vote.
The whole model
holds just sixteen learned
parameters, about one kilobyte,
and runs on an ordinary laptop.
Tested on sixty-five people
it had never seen,
and repeated ten times,
the Alzheimer's module reached
eighty-two point six percent
balanced accuracy, and correctly
cleared ninety-four percent
of healthy people.
Three electrodes beat the full nineteen.
The depression marker scored
fifty-five percent:
close to chance.
And it is a working prototype.
Upload a recording,
and within seconds
you get a plain-language
verdict, the vote of every slice,
the measurements behind
it, and a P D F
report.
NeuroDetect is a screening aid,
not a diagnosis.
Before it is used in clinics,
it needs testing on data from
other hospitals.
NeuroDetect.

more accessible.
