The question

Can a short brain-wave recording hint at Alzheimer's?

An EEG records the brain's electrical rhythm with small sensors on the scalp: painless, low-cost and already in most hospitals.

A healthy resting brain
Small, fast, regular waves: the alpha rhythm, about 10 per second
A brain affected by Alzheimer's
The rhythm slows: bigger, lazier waves — the theta rhythm, 4–8 per second
The problem

Alzheimer's is often recognised late

60–70%

of dementia cases are caused by Alzheimer's disease [WHO]
