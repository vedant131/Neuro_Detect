Can a short brain-wave recording hint at Alzheimer's?

NeuroDetect reads an EEG, the painless test where small sensors on the scalp record the brain's electrical rhythm. It looks for the slowing that Alzheimer's disease causes and shows, in plain words, what it found and why.

Analyse a recording See how it works
{% include "_trace.svg" %}
A healthy resting brain Small, fast, regular waves (the "alpha rhythm", about 10 per second).
A brain affected by Alzheimer's The rhythm slows down: bigger, lazier waves (the "theta rhythm", 4 to 8 per second).

What happens when you upload a recording

  1. Pick the useful sensors

    Out of the usual 19 scalp sensors, we only need 5: three for memory-related changes and two for mood.

  2. Clean the signal

    Background electrical noise is filtered out and the recording is cut into 10-second slices. Slices spoiled by blinking or movement are thrown away.

  3. Check every slice

    Each slice is compared with a typical healthy brain and a typical Alzheimer's brain, and votes for whichever it resembles more.

  4. Explain the result

    You get the vote count, a plain-language summary, and the exact measurements behind it, ready to save as a PDF.

Two checks from one recording

Memory check (Alzheimer's)

Uses the sensors at the back of the head (O1, O2) and the top-front (Fz). It measures two things doctors know change in Alzheimer's:

  • Slowing: how much slow theta rhythm there is compared with healthy alpha rhythm.
  • Teamwork: how well the front and back of the brain stay in step with each other.

Mood check (depression)

Depression in older adults can cause forgetfulness that looks like dementia. Because of that, the same recording is also checked for a left-right imbalance at the front of the brain (F3, F4), a pattern studied in depression.

Honest result: in our tests this brain-wave sign was not reliable enough on its own, so the report always asks for a short mood questionnaire (GDS-15 or PHQ-9) as well.

Why this matters

PainlessAn EEG is just sensors resting on the scalp: no needles, no radiation.
AffordableEEG machines are common in hospitals; brain scans like PET can cost many times more.
ExplainableEvery result shows which measurements pushed it one way or the other.

NeuroDetect is a screening aid for research. A result here is a reason to talk to a doctor, not a diagnosis.
